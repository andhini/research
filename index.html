Fluid dynamics in engineering and sciences

How to explain what we experience in our daily activities related to fluid flow?
From riding a bike, swimming, traveling in an airplane, we live and experience the sophisticated physics phenomena of fluid dynamics. Yet, few can understand and explain in detail how they affect our lives. Most importantly, how to use the knowledge of flow physics to make our lives easier and better.

Aquatic animals achieve high propulsive efficiency by exploiting fluid–structure interaction, a combination of flexible fin and body deformation in response to surrounding flow. These create a two-way coupling between motions to generate hydrodynamic forces. The dynamic interaction enables constructive vortex formation, efficient momentum transfer, and passive energy storage, allowing animals to generate thrust with a more efficient cost of transport than rigid mechanical propulsors.

    The boundary layer dynamics of a flapping foil are governed by unsteadiness of pressure gradients and non-equilibrium effects arising from its oscillatory motion. Unlike stationary foils, the periodic acceleration and deceleration modify separation, transition, and relaminarization processes, leading to complex interactions between the boundary layer, the oscillatory phases, and near wakes. These processes strongly influence the generation of lift, drag/thrust, and the overall aero/hydrodynamic performances.

Data assimilation from Direct Numerical Simulation (DNS) or experiments into a CFD solver enables systematic correction of coarse-grained simulations by incorporating high-fidelity flow information. By constraining large-scale dynamics while modeling unresolved scales, assimilation improves accuracy, stability, and physical consistency without incurring the full computational cost of fully resolved simulations.
